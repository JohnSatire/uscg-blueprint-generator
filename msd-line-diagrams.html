Diagram 1 — Existing / as-built

V-1 bottom suction → macerator → bookcell → V-2 → gravity pump → overboard. Bookcell Cl₂ varies with salinity (no control). Dilution seawater (~30%) enters at the gravity pump and drops overboard residual below bookcell exit — still typically over ADEC without dechlor.

Process steps — as-built

  1. Step 1 — Draw from V-1 bottom. Sewage + settled solids leave the ~1,000 gal holding tank at bottom suction. Why: bottom suction pulls solids into treatment instead of leaving them in the tank.
  2. Step 2 — Grind in the macerator. Solids are chopped and pumped toward the bookcell. Why: bookcell electrodes need a pumpable slurry, not large solids.
  3. Step 3 — Electrochlorinate in the bookcell. Flow rises through the cell; Cl₂ output tracks seawater salinity (no setpoint control). Why: as-built has no bleach dosing — residual varies (beads show ~10 / 15 / 20 ppm leaving the cell).
  4. Step 4 — Buffer in V-2. Treated flow enters the ~300 gal tank (with vent) before discharge. Why: short hold / surge volume between cell and overboard pump.
  5. Step 5 — Dilute and pump overboard. Gravity pump takes ~67% from V-2; ~30% seawater dilution joins at the pump; mixture exits the out sea chest. Why: dilution lowers residual (~7 / 10 / 13 ppm) but still often exceeds ADEC without dechlor.

Diagram 2 — Altered plant

Dual V-1 suction (+1′ duty / bottom clean-out), SW under macerator into a 2:1 mix tee → bookcell → V-2A/B welded plate with peri bleach (V-2A) and dechlor (V-2B) → gravity → Paramount UV2 → out. Mix beads show 2 SW : 1 sewage; residual Cl₂ leaves near ~2–3 ppm after dechlor.

Process steps — altered plant

  1. Step 1 — Dual suction on V-1. Duty draw at +1′ mid level; bottom path reserved for clean-out. Why: mid suction reduces solids plug risk during normal process; bottom stays for cleaning.
  2. Step 2 — Macerate sewage only. Macerator handles sewage; a separate SW pump pulls from the SW sea chest. Why: seawater is metered independently so the mix ratio can be controlled.
  3. Step 3 — Mix 2 SW : 1 sewage. Flows join at the mix tee before the bookcell. Why: stable salinity/conductivity improves electrochlorination predictability (animated beads show 2 blue : 1 yellow).
  4. Step 4 — Treat through bookcell → V-2A / V-2B. Welded-plate tank: peri bleach into V-2A, dechlor into V-2B. Why: raise then quench Cl₂ so overboard residual lands near ~2–3 ppm.
  5. Step 5 — Discharge via gravity + Paramount UV2. Gravity pump sends flow through UV2 to the out chest. Why: UV is a final barrier after chemical treatment for proposal compliance.

Diagram 3 — 208 V control + sterilize

Same altered plant in sterilize mode: macerator and V-1 idle; SW pump ON via 208 V contactor M1. Aux contacts feed peri bleach (ON) while dechlor is dialed to 1 so Cl₂ stays high (~16–20 ppm) through UV2 for system sterilization.

Process steps — sterilize (208 V)

  1. Step 1 — Idle sewage path. V-1 and macerator stay OFF; no sewage feed. Why: sterilize is a clean seawater / chemical loop, not a blackwater process run.
  2. Step 2 — Energize 208 V contactor M1. Coil pulls in SW pump power; aux contacts R1/R2 seal peri feeds. Why: one motor start also arms chemical pumps so sterilize chemistry cannot run without SW flow.
  3. Step 3 — SW-only through bookcell into V-2A/B. Seawater fills / circulates the altered plant train. Why: wets electrodes, piping, and tanks for a kill residual.
  4. Step 4 — Bleach ON, dechlor dialed to 1. Peri #1 doses 7% bleach to V-2A; dechlor is minimized so Cl₂ stays high (~16–20 ppm). Why: high residual is intentional for system sterilization, not overboard compliance.
  5. Step 5 — Hold through UV2 to out chest. High-Cl₂ water passes UV2 / sample point and can exit when authorized. Why: documents the sterilize flow path for USCG / plan review of the altered plant.

Diagram 4 — Transfer, wash & flush

Interactive valves · Backwash interlocks with process feed (V-FWD / V-SW-NORM) · Mix ratio 2 SW : 1 sewage. Click any valve; inactive pipes dim; beads follow open paths.

Operating steps — transfer / wash / flush

  1. Step 1 — Pick a mode. Use Process mode (normal treat), Backwash mode (bookcell reverse flush), or Reset defaults. Why: presets set the valve interlocks; you can still click any valve to what-if a lineup.
  2. Step 2 — Process lineup (typical). Open V-SW-NORM + V-FWD (+ often V1-MID and V-REC): SW and macerated sewage meet 2:1, through bookcell; backwash BC-FLUSH stays closed. Why: BC-FLUSH interlocks against process feed so you cannot forward-feed and reverse-flush at once.
  3. Step 3 — Transfer / tank moves. Use V-U (V-2A↔V-2B), V1/V2 suction valves to the macerator manifold, and MAC-V2 / V-REC as needed for moves and recirculation. Why: Diagram 4 is the rehearsal board for transfers without guessing pipe routes.
  4. Step 4 — Wash sprays. Open V-SW-V1 / V-SW-V2A / V-SW-V2B to spray seawater into tanks while watching bead paths. Why: confirms wash water source and which tank interiors are being rinsed.
  5. Step 5 — Backwash the bookcell. Press Backwash mode (opens BC-FLUSH, closes process feed). Confirm inactive pipes dim and only flush beads move. Why: reverse flush clears the cell without sending sewage forward.
  6. Step 6 — Treated overboard. Open V-GRAV and V-GRAV-OUT for gravity discharge (blue). Keep MAC-OUT / V1-TOP-DRN / V-OVF closed unless intentionally showing untreated (red) paths. Why: red paths are untreated emergencies — leave them closed in normal process demos.
